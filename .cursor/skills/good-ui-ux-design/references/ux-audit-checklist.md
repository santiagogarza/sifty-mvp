## 1. Understand User Behaviors

Always start with customer empathy:

- [ ] **Get 5 answers for each "General Empathy Question" (GEQs)** — Get 5 customer answers for each of the 3 Generic Empathy Questions. You can either (a) create a survey (e.g., Typeform) and send it to a handful of customers, or (b) talk directly to your customer (video chat, call). One video chat is often worth 100 survey answers.

- [ ] **Research your customers' Motivations** — What are your customers' hopes, pains, etc.?

- [ ] **Identify the top Motivation levers** — Identify the top 3 motivations that users have before/during/after using your product.

- [ ] **Research your customers' Abilities** — How much time, money, physical/mental capacity, and practice does your customer have with the context surrounding your product?

- [ ] **Identify the top Ability levers** — Identify the scarcest Ability resources at play before/during/after using your product. Does the user have the capabilities of performing the desired behavior? Your customer's ability is a function of the scarcest of the five ability levers at that moment.

- [ ] **Validate the Prompt** — Do you offer the user a clear nudge to act at the right moment? Without a prompt there's no action.

- [ ] **Sketch a 6P Story** — Use the previous customer insights to sketch a 6P story. How does your product fit in the real-life story of your customer? (Person, Problem, Promise, Process, Proof, Payoff)

- [ ] **Get 5 answers for each "Specific Empathy Question" (SEQs)** — Get 5 customer answers for each of the 3 Specific Empathy Questions so you can identify Behavior Blockers and Enablers for both successful and dropout customers.

- [ ] **Analyze the Psych variations of a screen** — Based on the SEQs customer insights, run a Psych analysis of your screen to see where the biggest drops in psych are.

## 2. Find the Gaps (B.I.A.S. Framework)

When looking for gaps in an experience, put yourself in your customers' shoes and start with these high-level questions:

### High-Level Check
- [ ] **Block**: Does this pass my brain filters?
- [ ] **Interpret**: What do I understand from this?
- [ ] **Act**: How can I take action?
- [ ] **Store**: Was the overall interaction satisfying? Was it clear and reassuring?

### B — Block: Help users see what's important
- [ ] Are things redundant? Or unrelated?
- [ ] Does it look like a high-effort commitment?
- [ ] Does it look like an ad? Is it placed close to an ad? Or at a usual ad location?
- [ ] Is there any unexpectedness to your experience?
- [ ] Validate the timing of your prompt. Is it aligned with people's behavior?
- [ ] Create value based on customers' hopes and pains to capture attention
- [ ] Create new, unexpected, and personalized touchpoints

### I — Interpret: Help users establish a good frame of reference
- [ ] Minimize cognitive load (visuals, text-heaviness)
- [ ] Build on familiar patterns
- [ ] Use anchors to compare (if elements can be evaluated)
- [ ] Turn waiting periods into value opportunities
- [ ] Make users' benefits clear (align them to their hopes)
- [ ] Improve discoverability of key actions
- [ ] Address people's fear of loss (tie it with benefits)

### A — Act: Help users achieve their goals
- [ ] Calculate your number of decisions per page
- [ ] Remove options (whenever possible)
- [ ] Create valid defaults to minimize user input
- [ ] Split into smaller steps
- [ ] Reveal features gradually

### S — Store: Make every interaction count
- [ ] Are you covering basic expectations from users?
- [ ] Are you providing clear feedback?
- [ ] Do users feel reassured when taking action?
- [ ] Are you showing signs that you care about users' outcome?
- [ ] Are there opportunities to delight on smaller interactions? (not just fancy animations — simple humanity often delights)
- [ ] Are you creating a positive relationship with users? (e.g., Are you the Shamwow guy or the holiday choral?)

## 3. Create Delightful Journeys

The key to a great customer journey is to observe its key components:

- [ ] **What's the root cause of your biggest Jump?** — Look for how that Jump relates to the context, the user motivations, etc.

- [ ] **What's the root cause of your biggest Drop?** — Pay attention not only to what's happening at that specific step, but slightly before it. Revisit the Motivation, Ability and Prompts from Module 1 and the B.I.A.S. Framework from Module 2.

- [ ] **How could you fill your Pit?** — Is it the result of a slow downwards slope or a sharp Drop? How could this be avoided?

- [ ] **How could you elevate your Peak?** — Think carefully about the customer's Motivations (Hopes, Fears, etc).

- [ ] **How well did you mark the Transitions?** — Is the clarity of the transition proportional to the importance of the milestone? Make sure users feel like every milestone is adequately celebrated.

- [ ] **How might you reorder some steps?** — Pay attention to what's happening around your Peaks, Pits, and Transitions. Could the experience be simpler and more memorable by shifting things around? Make sure users have enough psych energy to go through all journey steps and still finish on a high note (Peak-End Rule).

- [ ] **How might you leverage the customer's waiting periods?** — How might those waiting periods be shortened or used as an opportunity to educate, reassure, or delight? (Think: Labor Illusion Effect).

## 4. Communicate Product Decisions

To rally your team around important decisions, make sure you:

- [ ] Understand business goals
- [ ] Have a good story to highlight the problem
- [ ] Use the right vocabulary using psychological principles

Before your product review meeting, answer:
- [ ] What's the goal of the review?
- [ ] What do I want feedback on? (Creating guardrails)
- [ ] What problem does this solution solve? (Are they business problems?)
- [ ] How does it currently affect our users? (What's the story behind it?)
- [ ] Why is this solution better than the alternatives?

## 5. Create Ethical & Humane Products

### Regret Test
- [ ] If a user was in the room with you while you talk about your product... would you say the same things?
- [ ] If users knew what your team knows... would they take the same action?

### Black Mirror Test
- [ ] What would using your product "too much" look like?
- [ ] Who or what disappears if your feature becomes very (or "too") successful?
- [ ] List all potential negative side effects that could emerge from this feature
- [ ] Brainstorm on how to prevent those scenarios

### Extra Ethical Considerations
- [ ] **Scarcity Authenticity**: Are you using real scarcity or false urgency?
- [ ] **Defaults**: Are defaults set to the user's advantage? Or are you profiting from inaction due to status-quo bias?
- [ ] **Completion**: Do you offer real exit points to give users a feeling of completion?
- [ ] **Control**: Do you offer ways for users to control when and what they receive?

### Humane Principles
- [ ] Does your product help users save time? How can you make them save even more?
- [ ] Does your product value users' attention? Are you sending "false" notifications?
- [ ] Does your product reflect human values?
- [ ] How does your product help "push humanity forward"?

### The "In Real Life" Test
- [ ] Transform your screens and interactions into real people in your mind
- [ ] What would they look like? What would they say? How would they act?
- [ ] Is it a person you would want to know and hang out with?
- [ ] Remember: people prefer to nurture a positive relationship with technology
